Dynkin diagrams of the WBase algebras

Antonio Candiello · WBase: how it works and why

Every simple Lie algebra is described by a Dynkin diagram: one node for each simple root, a bond between two nodes when their roots are not orthogonal. Here the diagrams are numbered the way WBase reads them, so you know in which position to write each label of the highest weight.

For example, in E7 the fundamental 56 is (0 0 0 0 0 1 0) and the adjoint 133 is (1 0 0 0 0 0 0). All dimensions are computed with WBase V3.0 (command R).

  • 27 above the node: dimension of the fundamental representation, the irrep with 1 in that position and 0 elsewhere
  • 0 below the node: position of the label in the WBase input, from 0 to rank − 1
  • long root · short root · in double and triple bonds the arrow points to the short root

Isomorphisms. Some low-rank algebras coincide, with a different numbering of the nodes:

  • B2 = C2: the spinor (0 1) = 4 of B2 is the fundamental (1 0) of C2, and the vector (1 0) = 5 of B2 is (0 1) of C2;
  • A3 = D3, i.e. SU(4) ≅ SO(6): the middle node 1 of A3 is node 0 of D3;
  • E5 = D5: WBase accepts E5 as an alternative name;
  • D2 = A1 × A1 is not simple: WBase accepts it as a special case, but not A1 alone (the minimum rank is 2).

A2, on the other hand, is not isomorphic to B2: the two algebras have dimension 8 and 10.
